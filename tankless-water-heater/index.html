Tankless service we handle

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to leak or run out. That also means the unit works harder at the moment you open a tap, and it is sensitive to scale, venting and gas supply. Most tankless problems come from one of three sources: mineral buildup, a restricted vent or a sensor that has gone out of range.

We install new units, repair existing ones and flush them. If your unit is showing an error code, cycling between hot and cold or giving lukewarm water, it is usually one of the issues below.

Why flushing matters in this area

San Jose Water Company supplies most Saratoga homes. Its groundwater source reads roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply, so the mineral load in your water depends partly on which source feeds your street. Hardness leaves scale on the heat exchanger. A scaled exchanger loses efficiency, throws error codes and fails early.

A flush circulates a mild acid solution through the exchanger to dissolve the scale, then rinses it. Many manufacturers recommend annual service. If your home is on harder water, ask us whether a water softener makes sense, since it protects the heater and every fixture.

Tankless installation: what changes

Size the unit

We calculate the flow rate you need and the temperature rise from your incoming water, so the heater can deliver hot water to several fixtures at once.

Check gas and venting

Tankless gas units draw more gas than most tanks and use specific venting. Both may need upgrades, including a larger gas line.

Install and connect

The unit is mounted, vented and connected to gas or power and water, with a service valve kit so it can be flushed.

Test and explain

We fire the unit, check the temperature, confirm there is no gas leak and show you how to read the display and flush it.

PG&E delivers natural gas up to the meter. The piping beyond the meter is ours to size and test. If you ever smell gas, leave the house and call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. When replacing a failed tank with a tankless unit, our water heater replacement page explains what happens to the old tank, and the installation page covers the sizing and venting plan in more depth.

Tankless strengths

  • Hot water on demand with no standby loss.
  • Takes less floor and wall space.
  • Units often outlast tank models with proper service.
  • Good for households with steady, spread-out use.

Tankless limits

  • Higher installation cost.
  • May need gas line and vent upgrades.
  • Needs annual flushing in harder water.
  • Several fixtures at once can exceed the flow rating.

Safety checklist before any tankless service

Gas appliances follow strict rules, and a tankless unit is certified to gas appliance standards such as ANSI Z21.10.3 when it is made. It must be installed to the manufacturer's instructions and the California Plumbing Code (UPC-based).

  • If you smell gas, leave the building, then call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.
  • Never block, cap or alter the vent pipe on a tankless unit.
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm near the heater and outside the bedrooms.
  • Keep the area around the unit clear of stored items and chemicals.
