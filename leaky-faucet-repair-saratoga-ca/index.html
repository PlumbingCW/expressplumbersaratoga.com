Leaky Faucet Repair: A Saratoga, CA Homeowner Guide

Most drips come from a worn seal or cartridge. Find your faucet type first, then match the repair. Here is how, and when to stop.

  • Written for Saratoga homes
  • Local water and sewer facts
  • Plain-language steps.

By Saratoga Express Plumbing · Published October 15, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026

A dripping faucet wastes water and money, and the drip tends to get worse. The good news is that most faucet leaks are a worn rubber part, not a failed faucet. The repair takes under an hour once you know which type you have.

Start by shutting off the water at the angle stop valves under the sink. Turn them clockwise. Open the faucet to release pressure and plug the drain so a small screw does not disappear. Then work out which design you are dealing with.

Identify the faucet type

Four faucet designs and their usual drip sources
TypeHow to tellUsual cause of a drip
CompressionTwo handles, you tighten to shut it offWorn rubber washer or seat.
CartridgeOne or two handles that move smoothlyWorn cartridge or O-rings.
BallSingle handle over a rounded capWorn seals and springs.
Ceramic discSingle handle, quarter-turn feelCracked disc cylinder or seals.

Fixing a compression faucet

Remove the handle

Pry off the decorative cap, unscrew the handle and lift it off.

Loosen the packing nut

Use an adjustable wrench to unscrew the nut and lift out the stem.

Replace the washer

Take the stem to a hardware store so you match the washer and O-ring size exactly.

Reassemble and test

Put everything back, open the stop valves slowly and run the faucet.

Fixing a cartridge faucet

Cartridge faucets are the most common in newer kitchens and baths. Pop off the handle cap, remove the screw and lift off the handle. Under it is a retaining clip or nut that holds the cartridge. Pull the cartridge straight up, noting its orientation, and bring it to the store for a match.

Replace the cartridge and its O-rings together. A thin coat of plumber's grease on the O-rings helps them seat and last. Push the new cartridge in with the same orientation, reinstall the clip and handle and test for leaks at the spout and at the base.

Fixing ball and ceramic disc faucets

Ball faucets have a rounded cap and a single handle. Unscrew the cap with pliers, lift out the cam and the ball, and replace the inlet seals and springs. Repair kits come in one box and match the brand, so photograph the faucet before you head to the store.

Ceramic disc faucets use a sealed cylinder. If the cylinder is cracked or scored, replace it as a unit and the matching seals. These faucets rarely drip until the cylinder is damaged, which scale can cause. A kit for your brand usually costs less than a new faucet.

Common mistakes that turn a small drip into a bigger repair

Overtightening is the main one. Cranking a handle shut to stop a drip scores the seat and makes the drip worse. Using the wrong washer is another, since a washer that is almost the right size will not seal. Forgetting to close the stop valves floods the cabinet.

Finally, do not reuse a cracked O-ring. A new O-ring costs little. Replace them together and keep a spare set in a drawer.

Why new faucets flow less

CALGreen, California's green building standards code, limits new bathroom faucets to 1.2 gallons per minute. If a new faucet seems gentler than your old one, the flow limit is the reason, and a clean aerator keeps it consistent.

Why Saratoga faucets wear out faster than you might expect

San Jose Water Company supplies most Saratoga homes. Its water quality report lists hardness by source, and groundwater reads roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported surface supply. Which source reaches your tap depends on your street.

Practical meaning: mineral scale builds on cartridges, aerators and valve seats. A faucet on a harder supply may drip within a few years because scale scores the seals. Cleaning the aerator and soaking parts in vinegar often restores flow. If the cartridge face is scratched and pitted, replace it.

Older homes near the Village along Big Basin Way may have galvanized supply lines that shed rust into valves. If your faucet drips again soon after a repair, rust particles may be holding the seal open.

Sources: San Jose Water Company annual water quality report.

When a repair is not worth it

A faucet more than fifteen years old, one with a corroded body or one that leaks from the base is often cheaper to replace than to repair twice. Parts for discontinued models can also cost more than a new faucet.

If the leak is under the sink, tighten the supply nuts a quarter turn first. If water still sprays or weeps, replace the supply line. If the stop valve itself leaks, call a plumber, because forcing an old valve can flood the cabinet.
