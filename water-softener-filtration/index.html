Why water quality differs from street to street

Most Saratoga homes get water from San Jose Water Company. Its supply blends treated surface water and groundwater, and the sources differ in hardness. Groundwater runs roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported surface supply, so the water at your tap depends on which source feeds your street and how the system mixes it.

The way to know is to test. A basic hardness test strip shows whether scale is a worry. San Jose Water also publishes an annual water quality report with the hardness range by source. We do not quote a single number for Saratoga because it changes with the source, and a test of your own tap beats any average.

White crust on fixtures and glass

Scale from dissolved calcium and magnesium dries into a crust on faucets, showerheads and dishes.

Heater and appliance scale

Scale coats heating elements and tankless exchangers and shortens their life. See tankless water heater service.

Dry skin and stiff laundry

Mineral buildup affects how soap works. Many households notice the difference after softening.

Taste, odor or chlorine smell

A filter, not a softener, handles chlorine taste. Different problems call for different equipment.

Softeners do not filter water. They swap hardness minerals for sodium or potassium. Some households combine a softener with a carbon filter, and some add a reverse osmosis tap for drinking water. We explain what each does so you buy only what your tap needs.

Installing a softener

STEP 1

Test and size

We test hardness and estimate household use to choose a unit with enough capacity between regenerations.

STEP 2

Pick a location

The softener needs a drain nearby, power, a bypass and clear access for adding salt. Garages and utility closets are typical.

STEP 3

Install the unit

We connect the supply, drain and brine line, set the control head and program it to your hardness.

STEP 4

Hand off

We show you how to add salt, use the bypass and check the settings.

Check for local softener rules

Some California communities restrict salt-based softeners that discharge brine into sewers. Ask the City of Saratoga and the sanitation district that serves your address what rules apply before you buy. We can install a unit that suits what is allowed.

Service and repair

Softeners that stop regenerating, leave hard water or run constantly usually have a problem in the control valve, the brine tank or the resin. Common fixes include cleaning the injector, replacing a worn valve or replacing the resin. If a unit is more than about fifteen years old, replacement can cost less than repeated repairs.

We service common brands and tell you honestly when a unit is past saving. If scale is already in your pipes, that is a separate conversation about repiping or about slowing the damage going forward.

Look for certification marks

NSF/ANSI Standard 44 covers cation exchange water softeners, and NSF/ANSI Standards 42 and 53 cover filters that reduce taste, odor and certain contaminants. Ask any seller which certification a product holds and check the mark on the product itself. A product with no certification may still work, but you cannot verify its claims.
