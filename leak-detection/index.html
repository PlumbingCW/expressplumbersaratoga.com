Emergency Leak Detection in Saratoga, CA

A wet ceiling, a hissing wall or a water bill that doubled with no explanation. We find where the water is going without tearing up the house to guess.

  • 24/7 emergency line
  • Acoustic, thermal and pressure tools
  • Minimal-damage locating

Water that leaves a pipe does not always show itself. It soaks into a slab, runs inside a wall cavity or seeps into soil, and by the time a stain appears the damage has been growing for weeks. If you can hear water running when everything is off, or you see water coming through a ceiling, shut the main valve and call our 24/7 line.

Leak detection is the work of finding the exact spot before any wall is opened. That saves money on drywall and flooring, and it tells your insurer where the loss started. Below is how we narrow it down, what clues to look for, and what to do while you wait.

Clues that water is escaping somewhere

The meter moves with everything off

Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the small dial or flow indicator on the water meter. Movement means water is leaving the system somewhere.

A bill that jumped without a reason

A bill that climbs for a month or two with the same household usually means a leak, often a running toilet or an underground line.

Warm spots, damp carpet or hissing sounds

Warm patches on a floor can come from a hot water line leaking under a slab. A hiss in a wall points to pressurized supply pipe.

Musty smell, peeling paint or new stains

Moisture in a wall or ceiling shows up as discoloration and bubbling paint. Mold needs damp materials to grow, so a smell that lingers is a warning.

Do this right away

Fast action limits the damage and gives us a clean starting point.

  • Close the main water shutoff, usually near the street side of the house or by the meter, and see if the problem slows.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can do so safely. Never touch electrical equipment while standing in water.
  • Photograph stains and damage for your insurer, and note the date you first saw them.
  • Check the meter before and after the shutoff. A moving meter with the main closed means the leak is on the street side of the valve.

Where the water ends up in Saratoga homes

Many Saratoga houses were built in the postwar decades on slab foundations, and a good number have copper supply lines running through or under the concrete. Copper pinholes from the inside, especially where water is hard or aggressive. That is how a slab leak can run for months before the first warm spot appears. If you suspect water under the floor, see our slab leak repair page.

Hillside lots add another wrinkle. A leak in a line that runs across a slope can surface far from where it started, since water follows soil and gravel downhill. The lawn that stays wet near the street may have a break near the house. San Jose Water Company supplies most Saratoga homes, and its groundwater source reads roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply. Harder water leaves scale, and scale is part of why older metal lines fail.

Repair options after we find it

Spot repair

  • Cut out the damaged section and replace it.
  • Smallest opening in the wall or slab.
  • Good when the rest of the pipe is sound.
  • Usually finished in a single visit.

Reroute or repipe

  • Run new pipe around a slab or wall.
  • Better when the same line leaks repeatedly.
  • See repiping for whole-house options.
  • Takes longer but ends the repair cycle.

Start with the meter, then call

San Jose Water Company, which supplies most Saratoga homes, offers its customers guidance for finding leaks with the water meter. The test matches the first check we run on any call: shut off every fixture, then watch the dial or flow indicator. Separately, the California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) requires new water piping to be tested and proved tight before it is covered, so a leak in new work is a failed test, not an accepted risk.
