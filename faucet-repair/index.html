When a faucet problem cannot wait

A dripping faucet is an annoyance. A faucet that will not shut off, or a supply line that bursts under a sink, is an emergency. Look under the sink for the two angle stop valves, one for hot and one for cold, and turn each clockwise. If the water stops, you have bought time. If the valves are stuck or leaking, close the main shutoff and call us.

Most faucet calls come down to worn parts inside the faucet body, a failed seal or a corroded supply line. Fixes range from a ten-minute cartridge swap to replacing the faucet and the valves behind it.

The handle will not shut the water off

A worn cartridge or valve seat lets water pass even when the handle is closed. Shut the angle stops under the sink and call.

Spray or drip from the supply line

Braided supply lines can fail at the crimp. Closing the stop valve stops it, and a new line is a quick repair.

Leak at the base of the faucet

Worn O-rings or loose mounting hardware let water seep under the sink deck and into the cabinet.

Reduced flow or sputtering

Scale clogs aerators and valves. Cleaning or replacing the parts restores flow.

Why replacement sometimes beats repair

A faucet more than fifteen years old, one with a pitted finish, or one for which parts are no longer made is often cheaper to replace than to fix a second time. New faucets also save water. Modern fixtures meet current California flow limits, so replacing an older model can lower both your bill and your noise level.

When we replace a faucet, we also check the angle stop valves and supply lines under the sink. Replacing them at the same time takes little extra labor and avoids a second visit when an old valve fails the first time you try to use it.

Hard water and faucets in Saratoga

San Jose Water Company serves most Saratoga homes, and its groundwater source runs roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply. Hard water leaves scale in aerators and cartridges. Unscrew the aerator at the tip of your faucet every few months and rinse it. A white crust is scale, and it is a sign of why the flow has dropped.

Sink-related problems we handle

Besides faucets, we repair leaking drain traps, loose sink baskets, failed sprayers and sink strainers. A sink that drains slowly is a drain issue, handled on our drain cleaning page. A kitchen sink with a disposal falls under garbage disposal repair.

Older homes near the Village often have galvanized supply lines or shutoff valves that have not been turned in decades. Those valves sometimes break when we first try to close them. If we see that risk, we tell you before we touch the valve and explain what the repair involves.

California sets the flow limit on new faucets

CALGreen, California's green building standards code, limits new bathroom faucets to 1.2 gallons per minute and sets separate limits for kitchen faucets. A replacement that meets the code is the legal choice for new installations, and it uses less water than a faucet from the 1980s.
