Why homeowners schedule a sewer camera inspection

A sewer lateral is buried, so problems stay hidden until water backs up. A camera inspection turns an unknown into a recording. It tells you whether the pipe is sound, whether roots have found it and whether a stretch has sagged or cracked.

Most people call us for one of three reasons. They are buying or selling a home, they have had a backup and want to know why, or they are planning work like a remodel, a new driveway or a large tree removal near the line.

STEP 1

Locate the cleanout

We open the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation or at the property line. If there is none, we access the line through a toilet or a roof vent.

STEP 2

Run the camera

A flexible rod carries a waterproof camera down the lateral. We record the whole run and note the distance at each defect.

STEP 3

Locate the problem

A transmitter in the camera head lets us mark the ground above any defect, so repairs are planned in the right spot.

STEP 4

Review the video

We walk you through what we saw, what is minor and what needs action, and give you a copy of the footage.

Inspecting a home before you buy

A standard home inspection does not usually include the sewer lateral, which is why many buyers add a camera run. A cracked clay lateral or a collapsed section found after closing becomes your repair. Found before closing, it becomes a negotiation point.

Ask the seller or agent for access to the cleanout. If there is none, tell us when you book and we plan another entry point. Send your footage to your agent and attorney, and use it to understand what a repair might involve before you commit.

Saratoga lots and older laterals

Saratoga mixes older hillside homes with newer tract and custom construction. Older laterals may be clay, cast iron or Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe that tends to deform. Newer homes more often have plastic. The camera, not the age of the house alone, tells you what is actually in the ground.

Inspection versus cleaning

A camera inspection looks. It does not clear a blockage. If the line is fully blocked, we usually clear it first so the camera can travel through, which is a separate service covered on our sewer line cleaning page. If water is already backing up, call the emergency line and mention it so we bring the right equipment.

Sewer service in Saratoga is split between agencies. According to the Santa Clara County LAFCO service review, West Valley Sanitation District serves part of the city. If the camera shows a defect near the public connection, we help you understand where your responsibility ends and the district begins.
