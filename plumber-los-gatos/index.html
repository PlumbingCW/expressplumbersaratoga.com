What to do while you wait for a Los Gatos emergency plumber

STEP 1

Shut off the water

Find the main valve and close it by turning clockwise. For a water heater leak, close the cold water valve above the heater and switch the heater off or to pilot.

STEP 2

Stop using drains

For a backup, stop using sinks, toilets, the dishwasher and the washing machine. Water has nowhere to go.

STEP 3

Protect the area

Move valuables off the floor, lay towels at the edge of the water and photograph the damage for your insurer.

STEP 4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher your address, ZIP code and what you see. Mention if sewage is involved or if the gas smells.

If you smell gas

A gas leak is never a plumber-first call.

  • Leave the building at once and do not turn lights or appliances on or off.
  • Call 911 from outside, then PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.
  • Wait for the utility to clear the building before you call us for repairs.

Housing in Los Gatos and what it means for your pipes

Downtown Los Gatos holds some of the oldest buildings in the Town. Older properties may have galvanized steel supply lines, clay or cast iron drains and original shutoff valves that have not been turned in decades. When one of them fails, you want a plumber who knows to bring fittings for older pipe and who will not force a valve that may break.

The foothill neighborhoods grew in the decades after the war, and they carry their own concerns. Houses on hillsides sit above the sewer main, so gravity is on your side, but long laterals under mature trees pick up roots. Lines that run downhill from a high main can also see high water pressure, which strains fixtures and joints. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you what your house sees.

Los Gatos Creek runs through the Town beside Highway 17, and Vasona Park and Oak Meadow Park sit near the center. Homes near the creek corridor have lower lots and, in a wet winter, may see more groundwater around foundations and buried lines. A camera inspection helps locate trouble before it becomes an emergency. See sewer camera inspection for how that works.

Sewer, water and who is responsible

According to the Santa Clara County LAFCO service review, West Valley Sanitation District provides sewer collection to the Town of Los Gatos, along with Campbell, Monte Sereno and part of Saratoga. If a backup involves the public main, the district is the agency to call. A camera run from your cleanout shows whether the blockage is on your side of the connection.

San Jose Water manages the Los Gatos Creek and Saratoga Creek watersheds, according to its water quality report. Ask the Town of Los Gatos Building Division about permits for water heater replacements, repiping and sewer lateral work at your address, since requirements depend on the project.

Backup on your side of the line

  • Only your house is affected.
  • Several fixtures drain badly together.
  • A cleanout cable restores flow.
  • A camera shows cracks, roots or sags.

Backup in the public main

  • Neighbors have backups at the same time.
  • Sewage surfaces in more than one home.
  • The blockage sits beyond your connection.
  • The sanitation district needs to be called.
