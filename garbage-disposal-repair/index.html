If your garbage disposal is humming but not turning, or water is leaking from under the sink, turn off the power at the switch and the breaker before you reach in. Never put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Our 24/7 line can help you decide whether this is something to wait on or a sink you need to stop using tonight.

Disposals fail in predictable ways. They jam, they leak, they stop running or they send water back up into the second basin. Each points to a different fix, and sometimes the clog is not in the disposal at all.

Reading the symptoms

Humming but not spinning

A jam. Something is lodged between the flywheel and the shredder ring. A hex wrench in the bottom socket can turn the plate back and forth to free it.

Silent, no hum

The unit has lost power. Check the reset button on the underside and the breaker. A tripped motor overload is common after a jam.

Water leaking underneath

A leak at the top flange, the dishwasher inlet or the bottom seam. A bottom leak means the housing has failed and the unit needs replacement.

Sink drains slowly or backs up

A clog in the trap or branch line. If the second basin fills, the blockage is past the disposal. See drain cleaning.

What not to do

Disposals injure people and wreck motors when treated carelessly.

  • Do not reach into the disposal by hand, even with the power off.
  • Do not pour drain cleaner into a jammed disposal. It sits in the unit and splashes when the crew works.
  • Do not run the unit if it is leaking, because water can reach the motor and electrical connections.
  • Do not force grease, bones, fibrous peels or large amounts of rice and pasta through the disposal.

Installing a new disposal

A replacement disposal bolts to the sink flange and connects to the drain trap and the dishwasher line. Most sizes fit most sinks, but horsepower, noise and feed type vary. A continuous-feed model runs while you push scraps in. A batch-feed unit runs only when a stopper is in place. Larger units cost more and handle tougher scraps, but the best choice depends on how you cook.

We check the electrical connection, the dishwasher knockout plug and the drain. If a first-time install needs a new outlet or wiring, we tell you before the work starts. A sink without a disposal needs a switch and an outlet, which we can arrange.

Disposals in older and newer Saratoga kitchens

Kitchens in Saratoga range from older homes near the Village with original sink plumbing to newer custom kitchens with large island sinks. In older homes, the drain trap and the tailpiece often corrode, so we replace them when we swap the disposal. In newer kitchens with double basins and dishwashers, the dishwasher line and air gap are the usual leak points.

Hard water also plays a part. San Jose Water Company serves most Saratoga homes, and its groundwater runs roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply. Scale accumulates on the grinding parts and the drain. Running cold water while the unit operates and flushing afterward helps.
