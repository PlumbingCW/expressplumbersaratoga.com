Plumbing for remodels, additions and ADUs

New plumbing starts on paper. Before a wall is framed, the supply lines, drains and vents have to be planned so they fit the structure and meet code. Planning ahead avoids cutting new framing later and keeps inspections on schedule.

Saratoga homeowners call us for kitchen and bath remodels, laundry rooms added on a second floor, accessory dwelling units behind the main house and outdoor kitchens. Each brings different questions about supply size, drain slope and where the new line connects to the old one.

From rough-in to final

Review plans

We read drawings, check fixture counts and confirm the supply and drain sizes. If the plans do not exist yet, we sketch the plumbing scope with you.

Rough-in

Supply, drain and vent lines are installed before walls close, then pressure tested and inspected.

Set fixtures

After tile, cabinets and walls are finished, we set toilets, sinks, faucets and shower trim.

Final inspection

We coordinate the final inspection with the City of Saratoga building division and correct any items noted.

ADUs and capacity

Accessory dwelling units are increasingly common across the Santa Clara Valley. An ADU adds bathrooms, a kitchen and often a water heater, and all of that connects to a water service and a sewer lateral that were sized for the main house. We check the line sizes and condition and tell you if the lateral can handle the added flow.

A camera inspection of the existing lateral before an ADU is built is a good investment. It shows whether the pipe is sound enough to carry another dwelling, or whether replacement should happen while the yard is already torn up. See our sewer camera inspection page for how that works.

Permits and inspections come first

New plumbing and most major changes require a permit from the City of Saratoga building division. Rough-in inspection happens before walls close, and a final inspection follows. We coordinate both. Ask the city what applies to your address, since requirements depend on the scope.

Local conditions that shape installation

Hillside lots complicate drain routes. A bathroom added at the lower end of a sloped house may sit below the sewer connection and need a pump, while an upper-floor addition needs to vent properly. We look at the lay of the lot before we recommend a routing.

San Jose Water Company supplies most Saratoga homes, and its groundwater runs roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply. For a new build or a major remodel, that is a reason to consider a softener or filtration setup while the walls are open.

The code behind the inspection

The California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) sets pipe sizes, venting distances, drain slope and testing for new work. Inspectors check the rough-in against it before the walls close. We design to the code from the start, so the inspection confirms the work instead of finding problems in it.
