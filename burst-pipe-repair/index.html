Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Saratoga, CA

Water pouring from a ceiling, wall or yard needs a hand on the main shutoff first and a plumber second. Our 24/7 line is ready for both steps.

  • 24/7 emergency line
  • Water shutoff guidance by phone
  • Repair or replace the failed section
  • Clear price before work starts.

If a pipe has burst, find the main water shutoff and close it right now, then call us. Turn the valve clockwise until it stops. The main is usually at the street side of the house, in a utility box near the meter, or where the line enters the garage or basement. Every minute the water runs adds damage, and our dispatcher can talk you through the shutoff if you are not sure where it is.

Once the water is off, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, switch off the water heater and keep clear of any electrical equipment that is wet. Then we take over: finding the break, stopping the loss and planning the repair.

What to do in the first ten minutes

Shut the main

Close the main valve to stop the flow. If it is stuck, tell the dispatcher, and we will talk through your options.

Drain and protect

Open the lowest faucet to release pressure, turn off the water heater, and move valuables out of the wet area.

Make it safe

Avoid wet electrical outlets and appliances. If water is near the panel, stay clear and tell us when you call.

Record the damage

Photograph the burst, the water and the damaged items before cleanup. Your insurer will want them.

Why pipes burst

Corrosion and age

Old galvanized steel rusts from the inside until the wall thins and fails. Copper pinholes from the inside in aggressive water.

Pressure spikes

High street pressure or water hammer stresses joints. A pressure regulator that has failed can put your entire system under strain.

Freezing

Saratoga mornings seldom freeze for long, but an exposed line on a cold night can crack. Foothill houses with outside hose bibs and unheated crawlspaces are most at risk.

Physical damage

Digging, a nail in a wall or a tree root against a buried line can puncture or split a pipe.

Hillside homes carry additional pressure concerns. Lines that run downhill from a high main see higher static pressure at the low end. A pressure regulator and a gauge on the hose bib tell you whether your system is running within a safe range. We test pressure on every burst pipe call because a broken pipe is often a sign the whole line is under stress.

Do not wait on a slow weep

A small drip at a joint can be the first sign of a larger failure. If you see a damp spot at a fitting, a green crust on copper or a pinhole spray, call before it becomes a flood. Early repair usually costs far less than the repair plus water damage.

Water damage and who to call

A burst pipe leaves water in drywall, insulation and flooring. Plumbers stop the water and fix the pipe. Water damage restoration companies dry and repair the building. We can recommend next steps, but keep that distinction in mind when planning. Mold can begin growing within a couple of days in wet materials, so do not wait on drying.

Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden pipe bursts, including resulting damage, though the pipe itself may be the owner's cost. Do not clean up before you photograph. Keep the burst section if we remove it, since adjusters sometimes want to see it.

What the plumbing code says about pressure

The California Plumbing Code, which follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), calls for a pressure-reducing valve where static water pressure can exceed 80 psi. If a gauge on your hose bib reads above that, a regulator is a code item, not an upgrade. Ask us to check it on any burst pipe visit.
