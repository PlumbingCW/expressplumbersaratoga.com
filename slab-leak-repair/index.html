What a slab leak is and how to spot one

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs through or under a concrete foundation. In slab-on-grade homes, supply lines often run inside the slab or in the soil beneath it, so a pinhole or a failed joint has nowhere to go but into the ground.

Signs include a warm patch on the floor, the sound of running water when fixtures are off, a water bill that rises without explanation, damp baseboards and a meter that moves with everything shut. If you see active water, close the main and call our emergency line. For suspected leaks that are not yet damaging, we schedule a locate visit.

Why slab leaks happen to Saratoga-area homes

Many houses built in the mid-twentieth century in the Santa Clara Valley sit on concrete slabs with copper piping beneath them. Copper can develop pinholes from the inside over decades. The condition of the water speeds the process. San Jose Water Company supplies most of Saratoga, and its groundwater reads roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, and scale can speed pitting where water moves fast around a joint.

Movement matters too. Slabs shift and settle, and hillside soil can creep. A pipe that rubs against concrete as the slab moves wears through. Foothill lots with fill soil are more likely to settle unevenly. If your slab has new cracks or doors that now stick, mention it when you call.

Three ways to fix a slab leak

Spot repair through the slab

  • Break a small opening in the concrete over the leak.
  • Replace the damaged section of pipe.
  • Patch the concrete and restore the floor.
  • Best for one isolated failure on a sound line.

Reroute or repipe

  • Abandon the leaking line under the slab.
  • Run new pipe through the attic or walls.
  • Avoids further digging under the floor.
  • Best when multiple leaks or aging pipe are present.

Rerouting is often chosen when the pipe under the slab is old and has more than one weak point. You can read about whole-home options on our repiping page. If you are unsure whether the leak is under the slab at all, a leak detection visit confirms it first.

What happens during the repair

STEP 1

Confirm the leak

We test pressure, listen along the floor and use thermal imaging to isolate the line that is failing.

STEP 2

Mark the spot

The leak location is marked on the floor with a tolerance of a few inches, so the opening is as small as possible.

STEP 3

Open and repair

Where we cut the slab, we protect the surroundings, replace the damaged section and pressure test.

STEP 4

Restore and document

The concrete is patched. We give you the findings in writing, which your insurer may ask for.

Pressure testing after the repair

After any repair or reroute, the water piping is tested and proved tight, which is a requirement of the California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) for new and altered water piping. We pressure test before the slab is patched and again before we leave, so a second leak shows up while the floor is still open.
