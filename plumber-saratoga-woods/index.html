Plumbing for a small neighborhood with big gardens

Saratoga Woods is a small residential area in northern Saratoga. Wikipedia's entry for Saratoga places it northeast of the Golden Triangle, behind Prospect High School and north of Cox Avenue. A local real estate guide puts it between Saratoga Creek on the west and Saratoga Avenue on the east.

The same guide describes mostly single-story homes with large front gardens on wide, quiet streets. We use that description as an editorial angle only. It tells us where to focus advice, not what is wrong with any house.

Outdoor water

A large front garden usually means irrigation lines, hose bibs and outdoor shutoffs. A leak outside shows up as a wet patch, a soft lawn or a bill that climbs while indoor use stays flat.

Mature trees

Roots in older yards can find a joint in a sewer lateral. Slow drains in several fixtures, or gurgling, are the usual hint. A camera shows the line without digging.

Single-story layouts

Pipes in a one-story house often run through a crawl space or attic. Wet spots on ceilings or soft floors are cues to call early.

Leaks you cannot see

The quickest home test uses the water meter. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the dial. If it keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. The meter lid is usually near the curb, and a flashlight helps.

If the meter moves, shut the main valve and note whether it stops. That tells you whether the leak is inside the house or in the line outside. Our leak detection page explains how a plumber then finds the spot without opening walls at random.

Before a small problem gets bigger

STEP 1

Find the main valve

Know where it is and that it turns. A valve stuck for years is better found on a quiet day.

STEP 2

Check the water heater base

Look for rust, damp insulation or a puddle once a season.

STEP 3

Watch under sinks

A dry cabinet floor is the goal. Staining or swelling means a slow leak.

STEP 4

Keep drains clear

Hair catchers and grease in the trash prevent most clogs. See drain cleaning if one keeps coming back.

Schools, streets and getting your address right

Prospect High School is a useful landmark, and the neighborhood sits close to Saratoga Avenue and Cox Avenue. Several streets in this part of Saratoga share names with other roads in the area, so give your full address and ZIP 95070 when you call.

Sewer service in Saratoga is divided between West Valley Sanitation District and Cupertino Sanitary District, according to the Santa Clara County LAFCO service review. Ask the district which one covers your street. For a nearby neighborhood, read about plumbing in the Golden Triangle or Quito.
