Planning a water heater installation in Saratoga

A new installation differs from a swap because something changes. You may be adding a heater to an addition, moving it out of a closet, switching from electric to gas or choosing tankless for the first time. Each of those touches venting, gas, water and sometimes electrical work, so the plan matters more than the unit.

We start with how your household uses hot water. A family of five with two showers running at breakfast needs different capacity than a couple in a hillside cottage. From there we look at where the heater will live, what it will vent through and whether your gas line and panel can support it.

Sizing, venting and gas

Tank size is chosen by the number of people and fixtures. Tankless sizing uses flow rate in gallons per minute and the temperature rise from your incoming water. Undersized units run out of hot water. Oversized ones waste money and, for gas models, may need bigger venting than your home has.

Gas appliances need a safe path for exhaust and a gas line that can deliver enough BTUs. A tankless unit may draw more gas than the tank it replaces, which can mean a bigger pipe from the meter. PG&E owns the line up to the meter, and we handle the piping on your side. If you ever smell gas during or after any project, leave the house and call 911 and PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.

Why homeowners pick a tank

  • Lower equipment and installation cost.
  • Simple to service and repair.
  • Works with existing vents in most homes.

Why homeowners pick tankless or heat pump

  • Less standby heat loss and more floor space.
  • Longer service life for tankless units.
  • Heat pumps cut energy use sharply in the right space.

Seismic bracing and code

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped to resist falling or horizontal movement. Saratoga lies in the Santa Cruz Mountains foothills near the San Andreas fault, so a strapped tank is one less thing to worry about after a shake.

A new installation usually needs a permit. The City of Saratoga building division is the place to ask about your address, and we can pull the permit and schedule the inspection for you. Expansion tanks, pressure relief valves and drain lines are added or corrected as part of the work.

Water quality and long-term care

San Jose Water Company supplies most Saratoga homes, and the groundwater it uses is roughly three times harder than Valley Water imported supply. Hardness leaves scale on heating elements and tankless heat exchangers. A yearly flush for tanks and a descaling service for tankless units keep both running closer to their rated life.

If scale is a problem in your home, ask about a water softener. Softening can protect the heater and the fixtures downstream.
